**Published:** 16 June 2026

Brazilian container ports may offer less carrier optionality than procurement models often assume.

I ran an HHI analysis of carrier concentration across the 10 main Brazilian container terminals using ANTAQ 2025 data.

Based on identifiable deep-sea container calls, 7 of the 10 terminals are highly concentrated under DOJ/FTC 2010 thresholds. The other 3 are moderately concentrated.

None falls into the unconcentrated range.

The extremes set the picture.

Fortaleza reaches an HHI of 5,679, with CMA CGM representing 74% of identifiable calls.

Santos, Brazil's largest container gateway, is the least concentrated in the group — yet still sits at HHI 2,057.

At the most concentrated terminals — Fortaleza, Itapoá and Portonave — a single carrier dominates the call pattern.

That does not mean shippers have no alternatives.

But it does mean carrier choice at terminal level may be structurally narrower than the headline "active carriers" count suggests.

After several years on the operational side of African ports — Luanda, Maputo, Beira and Nacala — I know this is not an abstract index.

High concentration can translate into less negotiation room, route planning that depends on one or two key relationships, and a dominant carrier becoming the reference option whether shippers prefer it or not.

For shippers, freight forwarders and procurement teams, the implication is practical:

Port selection should not be separated from carrier optionality.

The real question is not only:

Which port is cheaper?

It is also:

Which port gives us real carrier optionality?

For anyone working in ocean freight, procurement or trade-lane management: do you actively factor port-level carrier concentration into terminal selection, or has it become an accepted feature of the Brazilian market?

Method, data and breakdown: https://github.com/hugopedro-ds/brazilian-maritime-analysis-2025

---

**Methodology note:** HHI calculated from 705 identifiable deep-sea container calls in ANTAQ 2025. This is call-based concentration, not TEU-weighted cargo share. It measures operational carrier presence, not actual booked cargo. Thresholds: DOJ/FTC 2010 guidelines; under the stricter 2023 update, all 10 terminals would qualify as highly concentrated.
